# H.E.X. gameplay overhaul design — 15 October 2026

**Planning only.** This draft audits current source and proposes a complete direction; it does not authorize implementation. No engine, test, benchmark, capture, cleanup, or canonical edit was performed for this audit. The qualified baseline is `p61-d6d1bfb9c529`, with 6459 as fallback. Its isolated stationary-movement improvement is not a current whole-game performance result. Preserve the dirty tree, saves, stable wires, previous packages, and rejected-experiment evidence. The user selected **illustrated 2.5D with smooth animated sprites**; the gameplay contract does not require a new 3D simulation.

## 1. Diagnosis: what is known and what is not

The request that hits/AoE feel random is a valid usability report. The inspected contact code is deterministic; this review did not find a random hit-selection rule. Current continuous contact ordering uses path fraction, collision priority, and stable IDs. That does not prove the result is easy to predict visually, fair in shared-pool exhaustion, or correct in every untested interaction.

The likely clarity burden is the combination of several different rules:

| Verified current behavior | Why it can look inconsistent | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| A projectile body uses swept planar circle contact; targets contribute their hurt radius. | A body edge can be hit while its center appears outside a visible ring. | Keep a consistent ground/foot anchor and distinguish contact boundary from harmless glow. Explain edge contact in the optional Range inspection. |
| Direct blasts/fans use analytic contact tests, while reaction selection stores whole positive-area hexes and those cells become the final effect authority. | A visually circular selection can produce a stepped damaging edge; its small outer cells matter. | Render the final stored edge for persistent residue, never a smooth circle that promises a different hit region. |
| Heavy's terminal blast quotes damage per eligible target. Fuse and Mantle consume a finite shared pool in entity order. | Two apparently equal targets can receive unequal damage when a shared pool empties; Heavy crowds behave differently. | Treat this as an explicit mechanical redesign, not a paint fix. Adopt the simultaneous allocation contract in section 3 only through a new rules/content version. |
| Clash depletion scales direct damage linearly and blast radius by square root of remaining strength. | An apparently similar spell can land with a smaller area after an interception. | Show weakened core/footprint before contact and use the actual remaining stock for the boundary. |

## 2. One visual grammar, all eight deliveries and all 63 mixtures

Every spell has three distinct visual layers:

1. **Contact core:** a clean circular luminous body for traveling orbs. Its solid extent conveys the actual contact radius; a small directional tail gives travel direction even in a still image. Use controlled brightness, not an opaque white bloom that covers a character.
2. **Harmless radiance:** soft halo, sparks and tiny trails may extend outside the core, but they use a consistently softer value/opacity and never define damage. Reduced effects reduces this layer first.
3. **Ground consequence:** a ground-anchored boundary generated from the exact analytic area or final stored hex coverage. A separated, open warning edge means forming; a closed edge and restrained pulse means active; a broken fading edge means harmless decay. A shape alone does not imply imminent damage if the source is dormant, depleted, protected, or only material setup.

Critical cues survive Low quality, reduced effects, dark/light terrain, large bodies, camera zoom, overlapping allies, and UI scale. Color is reinforced by edge pattern and motion. A hostile boundary uses a consistent danger treatment; ownership/team tint is secondary to element identity. Purely decorative marks cannot be the only distinction between one and several independently damaging products.

This follows Riot's public clarity priorities: understandable consequences, attention proportional to gameplay importance, minimal noise, recognizable silhouettes, and effects that align with hitboxes. It does not import League's numerical balance or artwork. [Riot: Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/).

## 3. Proposed mechanical revision: fair finite AoE allocation

**This section is a breaking proposal, not current behavior or approved tuning.** Visual truth can ship independently. If the overhaul changes allocation, version the content/simulation protocol and requalify saves/replays/network identities; never reinterpret old recordings silently.

The proposed ruleset resolves one pulse as a batch:

1. At its defined damage phase, freeze eligible targets, their relevant state, the final area, and cover witnesses. Use the same frozen physical cover for every target; breaking cover during that pulse cannot reveal a later target to that same pulse. Re-evaluate at the next pulse.
2. Apply owner/team/dead/protection/height/previous-contact exclusions before allocation. Resolve contact priority deterministically: physical obstruction wins an exact travel tie; explicitly documented endpoint contact remains allowed. No change to body order may change which simultaneous equal target gets the pool.
3. Compute each target's capped requested **actual Health loss** after its mitigation and lethal clamp using a pure bounded integer quote over the frozen phase. For demands `d_i`, total `D`, and available pool `B`, allocate `floor(d_i × min(B,D) / D)` in existing fixed-point Health units when `D>0`; otherwise allocate zero. Use checked bounded integer/rational arithmetic, never float scaling. Retain the remainder only in this same live cast/product for a later eligible pulse; expire it at the original terminal lifetime. Fuse's terminal residue expires immediately after its sole pulse. Do not distribute residue by entity ID, extend a lifetime, or transfer it into a new source. Equal contemporaneous targets receive equal loss, with only unavoidable sub-unit remainder left unspent.
4. Debit only actual applied Health loss; record pool-before/after and per-target allocations. The commit must apply the planned post-mitigation loss exactly once rather than passing it through mitigation or a minimum-one raw-damage rule again. Zero allocation causes no Health loss, no debit, and no damage-derived status/hit confirmation. Under this proposal it does **not** consume Mantle's one-positive-contact marker; the original lifetime still advances and exhaustion grants no free retry stock. Any independent zero-damage control effect would need its own explicit authored eligibility and is not introduced here. Target removal, protection, mitigation, or a lethal clamp cannot create stock. All targets in the batch use the same phase snapshot; subsequent effects observe the committed result.
5. Mantle's contacts occurring at different times still consume the remaining cast pool chronologically. A target arriving later is not simultaneous and is not entitled to already spent damage. Conflagrations from distinct paid sources remain distinct products; do not merge their timers or damage simply because their pixels overlap.

**Tuning examples, not proposed shipped numbers:** a 20-Health area pool against two equal 12-Health demands gives 10 each; against demands of 6 and 12 it gives 6 and 12 and leaves 2. Three equal demands sharing 20 receive equal fixed-point floors, with the remainder retained/expired. A protected target is excluded rather than consuming a share. These examples are allocation acceptance cases, not blanket damage buffs/nerfs.

For a fully uniform new model, author separate `direct_cap`, `area_pool`, `per_target_cap`, status stock and material stock in the compiled delivery. Heavy's primary direct hit must not also receive its splash unless the new rule explicitly says so. Migrating Heavy's current per-target splash to an authored finite area pool is a deliberate crowd-damage change requiring separate one/two/five-target tests and playtests. Spray pulse allocation and repeated ground pulses must use declared budgets rather than inheriting an accidental per-target rule. Do not invent numeric totals before recording current paid outcomes and choosing the desired time-to-defeat and resource tradeoff.

This uniform area model is recommended for evaluation because the rule is teachable: **the marked area shows who can be affected; the cast has a finite pool shared fairly among simultaneous eligible targets**. It is not automatically better balance: additional enemy bodies can dilute a shared pulse and create body-shielding incentives, and a crowd-clearing Heavy loses strength if its old per-target damage is simply placed into a small shared pool. Require one/two/five-target role and counterplay acceptance before selecting budgets. If the model fails the intended area-control role, retain the current per-target Heavy category explicitly rather than hiding a special case behind identical effects. Root/design signoff chooses this fork before implementation.

## 4. Movement, animation and defense

Current fullbody presentation already selects jumpsquat, jump/fall/land, slide, wall-run, cast and recovery states. It also uses locomotion phase and wall travel; do not claim these states are absent. There is a source-confirmed authoring gap: the pixel presenter explicitly marks strafe/backpedal art incomplete, reuses forward/shared-neutral walk keys, and labels several advanced-action poses as rigid placeholders. Authored cycles and transition work are justified; further visual review determines their quality, not whether those missing keys exist. [Incomplete anatomy and fallback declarations](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/pixel_flipbook_presenter.gd:13), [actual fallback metadata](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/pixel_flipbook_presenter.gd:326), [action selection](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/fullbody_cast_presenter.gd:302), [phase and attached wall-run](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/fullbody_cast_presenter.gd:344).

The animation contract is a stable foot/ground anchor, an independently legible facing direction, and visible preparation → action → recovery. Interpolate presentation between fixed 120 Hz states; never interpolate damage eligibility, payment, collision, or contact time. Body/robe follow-through can continue during recovery but may not hide the feet or imply an extra dodge. Cast upper-body motion should coexist with travel without changing the legal movement/cast cancel windows. Hit confirmation occurs on actual impact, not at the most dramatic animation frame.

Teach one short chain with the existing controls: move, sprint, slide, **press then release Jump**, land; then demonstrate projectile avoidance versus a ground blast and one actual paid wall-run. Keep the existing optional lessons, remapped button names, camera-look-ahead toggle, and reduced-effects setting. A defensive action must show whether it changes body contact, timed protection, movement only, or height. A Mantle remains an attack. Wall-run remains exposed where current rules say it is.

Acceptance: real held/pressed/released input through the router; no resource grants; front/side/back approaches; neutral input, exhaustion, active status, death/cancel, modal/focus return, controller and keyboard. Confirm fullbody pose and hurt anchor at release/contact/landing; replay authority must be identical under all presentation settings. Professional motion quality requires actual viewing, not just valid atlas hashes. Riot's discussion of silhouette and animation remaining inside a readable gameplay identity is the relevant precedent. [Riot: Modernizing the Monk](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/dev-modernizing-the-monk/).

## 5. Thirty characters, 21 ancestries, 51 passives

Keep the existing roster and earned data. Current passives are deterministic conditional ratios over authoritative state, bounded 0.75–1.35, with ancestry and character stacked in a defined order. Positive costs/damage cannot round to free/immune. Paired startup/cooldown bargains are suppressed if tick quantization would grant one side without its cost. This is useful design infrastructure, not proof that every passive is naturally useful or all characters are balanced. Improve selection into a concise playstyle promise: one character passive, one ancestry passive, body/defense implications, and one short ordinary action example. In the Range or end-of-drill summary, show actual activation count/duration and concrete resulting cost/timer/damage difference; avoid a permanent scrolling passive HUD. Suppressed or inactive timing perks should be explained truthfully. Do not claim an observed timer duration is additional damage or guaranteed value.

Balance audit uses natural trigger frequency plus paired finite encounters, both favorable and unfavorable matchups, low-resource windows, and three body sizes. Check flight/double-jump access as route tradeoffs, not interchangeable cosmetic variants. Preserve distinct identities, but revise a passive only when its trigger is effectively unreachable, misleading, one-sided after quantization, or repeatedly dominates equivalent opportunity. No blanket speed/damage increase and no new grind tree in the first slice.

## 6. Enemies, learning, progression and the core loop

Current signature rivals already have per-delivery windup/pressure/recovery profiles, committed aim, pauses without new casts, and finite actual payment. Existing shots/fields remain dangerous during the rival's recovery. Preserve that distinction. [Rival profiles](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/app/signature_rival_policy.gd:8), [commitment and commands](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/app/signature_challenge_session.gd:75). Reward admission already requires a clean owned paid victory and separates developer practice; do not replace it with menu completion. [Reward admission](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/app/signature_challenge_session.gd:103).

The overhaul loop should be **choose a small objective → encounter one readable problem → earn/use a tool → return to a clearly named next objective**. The current Rapid → Fuse → Rebound three-lesson route is a good vertical slice, not a full campaign. Refine those three existing lessons before adding more rivals. Each gets one environmental use of its reward, one alternate counter, and a rematch with an explicit challenge condition. An optional next expansion is three authored district boss lessons plus one small connected interior, not seven districts full of new quests at once.

Use enemy roles with distinct timing and geometry: committed line caster, delayed area setter, close pursuer, bank-shot specialist. Increase difficulty through combinations, positioning and smaller recoverable decision windows, not hidden aim tracking, free resources, inflated damage, or unreadable overlap. AI reads legitimate observation and committed information; any extra AI information is a declared difficulty rule with a visible consequence.

Range improvements build on the existing 504-spell tool: tasks such as “see why this missed,” “avoid a body shot but not a ground pulse,” and “compare this passive while recovering.” One removable result card can list direct hit, splash, status loss, ground loss, refused cast reason, and resource spent from actual events. Do not invent exact miss reasons the current events do not record; add that diagnostic event contract deliberately before showing them. Existing motion/return-fire/body-size controls and status seconds remain useful, not features to recreate.

Apex's official Range redesign supports specific repeatable practice and configurable combat targets; Oracle of Seasons demonstrates a small learned environmental rule opening a route. Gungeon's official description ties movement, dodge and usable cover to a clear combat loop. These are scoped precedents, not requests to copy full systems. [EA: Arsenal Range redesign](https://www.ea.com/fr-fr/inside-ea/news/arsenal-season-game-updates), [Nintendo: Oracle of Seasons](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Game-Boy-Color/The-Legend-of-Zelda-Oracle-of-Seasons-266263.html), [developer/publisher Steam page: Enter the Gungeon](https://store.steampowered.com/app/311690/Enter_the_Gungeon/).

## 7. Large walls, interiors, tactical visibility and camera

The recent architectural skin adds massing inside existing footprints; it is not physical elevation or enterable space. Current art-camera validation caps facade sizes and current nonpractice doors are visual props. Source: [kit validation](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/wellspring_illustrated_kit.gd:206), [roof/door drawing](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/wellspring_illustrated_kit.gd:677). The living viewpoint body already has an explicit redraw above the mask, including spectator ownership rules. [Owned-body overlay](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/app/bootstrap.gd:4124). The user asks for a reliable visible self, not evidence that this mechanism is absent.

Proposed art/space contract: map walls and building facades, including practice walls, read at least twice the largest approved character's opaque standing height at the same projection/zoom. Freeze `Hlarge` from feet to crown, excluding jump, halo, shadow and atlas padding. Current scale references need reconciliation: the pixel presenter uses a nominal large height of 68 px while [body profile validation](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/content/body_type_profile_catalog.gd:67) expects a 76 px large visual template; these imply 136 or 152 px respectively and are examples, not a final new-art target. Any low-cover exception requires an explicit design decision and unmistakably different identity; do not silently exempt today's shallow practice walls from the user's request. A parapet or ledge must not look climbable unless an actual route exists. Tall visual mass can be implemented before vertical physics, but cannot claim to stop/jump-over projectiles by height until that collision model exists.

Use a small set of authored physical classes—explicitly approved low cover, full blocker, wall-run surface, doorway/portal—and publish their movement, projectile, reaction and LOS behavior. A true enterable building requires a connected navigable interior, real collision/door aperture, deterministic room identity and transition rules, and a roof/wall cutaway that preserves cover. A painted dark rectangle is not a doorway. Prefer the first room to share one continuous planar simulation: an open aperture passes ordinary actors/projectiles/material under existing contact rules; a solid closed door blocks according to its authored collision class; a field remains fixed in world space and is clipped only by the rule used when it forms. Dynamic doors require a separate decision about existing fields rather than silent recomputation. Unloaded-room transitions cannot duplicate or discard paid stock. Multi-floor authority is outside this first room.

Always retain the living self's actual silhouette/ground marker where foreground art would hide it. Fade/cut away only the obstructing visual layers; never reveal an enemy, hidden projectile, interior, or ground hazard that the gameplay visibility policy excludes. Repainting the self does not make a hostile attack visible through cover. Test large-body front/back/corner approaches, wall-run, jump, dead spectator handoff, smoke/concealment, camera limits and 200% UI scale. Keep stable camera framing and the independent look-ahead option; do not add automatic camera swings to compensate for bad composition.

Introduce one finished building/interior in the three-lesson vertical slice, with approach, doorway, room combat, return and persistence. Then expand the world only after its rule vocabulary is understood. Actual vertical floors/platforms, projectile elevation and destructible architecture are separate expansions with new authority, pathfinding and network cost—not texture tasks.

## 8. Multiplayer and balance boundaries

Preserve authoritative 120 Hz inputs, deterministic integer rules, paid admission, finite material/source identity, and snapshot validation. Rendering may predict motion, but a hit confirmation, damage number, unlock or protection break must correspond to an authoritative event. Improved animation never changes collision merely by moving pixels. Local cutaways cannot leak opposing actors or tactical state.

For a rules revision, explicitly version allocation, height, room/door state, footprints, event reasons and compiled content; reject incompatible peers and preserve old portable saves/backups. Test real host/client input, admission/refusal, impact, shared-pool allocation, reconnect, interrupted room crossing, save/reload and simultaneous hits. Isolated replay parity is necessary but not proof of network feel or plug-and-play hosting. Do not promise automatic internet matchmaking or public deployment as part of a local visual overhaul.

Balance reports separate single-target time-to-defeat, two/five-target area allocation, finite resource efficiency, crowd control uptime, recovery opportunity, travel/cover access, and actual reaction setup cost. Use ordinary recovery/cooldowns and realistic rival pressure. No free refills in gameplay evidence; explicitly labelled prepared boundary controls are complementary. Do not equate six status icons or larger ingredient count with six times the damage.
