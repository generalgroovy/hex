**Recommendation.** Keep the deterministic planar simulation, finite paid spell authority, working portable pipeline, and reviewed ownership boundaries. Rebuild the player-facing presentation and world/content contracts around one complete vertical slice: one hero with finished motion, one distinct glowing projectile, one explainable area effect, one tall enterable building, and a useful room. Use that slice to choose an affordable art implementation and prove actual frame pacing. Do not promise that a global renderer change, more caches, or a full 3D conversion will make the game faster.

**Performance and renderer strategy.** First establish ordinary packaged d6d1 pacing through the desktop front door, then calibrate the same workload with light and full observation. The current [Profile route](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/scripts/launch-hex.ps1:71) invokes the source profiler, not the portable executable. Source observer evidence must stay labelled source; a matched packaged observer requires a separately qualified diagnostic package or a suitable external measurement path. Do not claim that observer mode already exists inside the released portable. Use the user's normal PC/app load as one declared environment, plus a separate reproducible reference environment if available. Do not close user applications, change power settings, or hide memory pressure to obtain a flattering number. Match quality, resolution, roster, spawn, paid spells, duration, focus, cap, build and backend; alternate process order. Preserve first-use and warm results and failed/unfocused runs. CPU elapsed timings are not thread CPU counters. Report GPU timing only where the backend supplies valid measurements. OS presented-frame evidence and engine intervals must remain distinct.

Godot's official guidance distinguishes steady frame cost, spikes and loading, and recommends finding the limiting stage before optimizing. CPU and GPU can overlap; their durations are not simply added. This supports measurement-first design, not a promise about H.E.X.'s bottleneck. [Godot general optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/general_optimization.html)

Keep Compatibility as the initial baseline (`project.godot:41`) while comparing the vertical slice. Test a renderer change as a separate controlled experiment only if the accepted art needs it or measured GPU/driver behavior justifies it. Budget opaque/static world work separately from dynamic spell layers, roof cutaways and translucent halos. Share compatible atlas/material resources while preserving painter order, per-view culling and threat information. Do not merge the whole campus into a single cache or globally sort transparent effects by material. Batching and transparency have different costs; Godot specifically warns about overlapping transparent work and culling tradeoffs. [Godot GPU optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/gpu_optimization.html)

The render-state seam should expose bounded actor poses, exact spell regions, owner/team/danger, material/phase, authoritative contact anchors and world/room visibility to presenters. Derive it once at a defined tick/view boundary, then interpolate only allowed presentation fields. It must not duplicate mutable authority or suppress live phase/owner changes. Renderer-owned caches require explicit source identity, invalidation, byte ceilings and teardown. Existing cache experiments failed whole-workload gates; creating a universal cache framework is not the first stage.

Use source/profile evidence to decide whether to change simulation layout or move one proven hotspot to native code. Preserve order-sensitive finite material allocation, status, movement, admission and contact order. Five current actors are not Riot's hundreds of minions: a flow-field or ECS rewrite has no demonstrated benefit here. The transferable Swarm lesson is to profile the actual scale, simplify only eligible representations, and retest the client after server/simulation work; Riot still found rendering/particles limiting after other optimizations. [Riot: The Tech Behind Swarm](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/the-tech-behind-swarm)

**Art and animation pipeline.** The user has selected illustrated 2.5D with smooth animated sprites. This is the fixed visual target. Compare two sprite implementations of the same accepted hero: a 2D skeletal rig with bounded hand-authored corrective poses, and an offline 3D rig baked into directional 2D sprite clips with artist cleanup. Both keep planar authority and the same illustrated style. Evaluate identity, eight-direction motion, cast readability, production effort, resident memory and real frame cost. Live 3D gameplay/rendering is not the proposed direction. Pixel snapping/filtering and smooth illustrated motion need a deliberate consistent art decision; mixing independently scaled pixel sheets, mesh deformation and blurred effects is not a coherent target.

Create one content specification for feet pivot, standing height, aim/travel directions, silhouettes, action names, anticipation/release/recovery events, cancellation, loop policy, attachment sockets and fallback coverage. Existing fullbody caches/residency are useful starting points ([Fullbody presenter](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/presentation/fullbody_cast_presenter.gd:43)); do not remove them to preload every future clip. Tie release/impact cues to authoritative events, not arbitrary animation completion. Animation anticipation may begin on accepted local prediction, but confirmed damage, debit and progression remain authoritative. Reset interpolation on teleport/room transfer/reconciliation discontinuities; Godot documents teleport reset requirements, but H.E.X. must apply equivalent reset to its custom history rather than adding engine interpolation blindly. [Godot interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_quick_start_guide.html)

Memory is a design constraint, not an export afterthought. An uncompressed 128×128 RGBA8 frame is 64 KiB. Twelve motions × eight directions × eight frames is 48 MiB per hero, 144 MiB for three, and 1.406 GiB for thirty, before mipmaps, duplicate CPU images or GPU working copies. Doubling both dimensions quadruples that. A 1024×512 RGBA8 page is 2 MiB; thirty pages are 60 MiB, both current cast and action manifests contain 30 characters (120 MiB for both full sets), but action-state coverage is incomplete and bounded residency means catalog totals are not measured residency. Use trimmed frames with fixed logical pivots, tightly packed bounded atlases, shared nonidentity effect assets, explicit clip reuse and per-scene residency. The proposed 64 MiB character envelope cannot hold three complete 48 MiB motion libraries, much less 15 admitted identities. That is an explicit pipeline rejection test: demonstrate skeletal reuse, trimmed frames and needed-clip residency with no visible pop or first-cast stall before accepting the budget. Do not reduce every hero to reused distorted poses just to meet a texture cap. Compare offline rig-baked output and 2D skeletal deformation costs using identical visual requirements; skeletal skinning must not distort silhouettes, feet, hands or the authored illustration.

The import/bake contract should include source license/provenance, approved original, tool/version/configuration, frame geometry/alpha/pivots, action coverage, palette, texture format and deterministic manifest hash. Keep lossless artist masters separate from runtime derivatives. Validate atlas bounds, missing actions, unintended interpolation, scale consistency and texture residency at build time. Artists need a one-command preview/contact sheet and an in-game camera/lighting comparison; avoid a new bespoke editor until existing Godot import/editor tooling proves insufficient.

**World scale, occlusion and real interiors.** Define `H_large` as opaque feet-to-crown height of the largest standing hero at the same camera/zoom, excluding optional glow. Current pixel art declares a nominal large body of 68 pixels (136 for twice that), while [body-type catalog](C:/Users/sende/Projects/hex/src/content/body_type_profile_catalog.gd:67) declares large visual height 76 (152 for twice that). These are inconsistent reference scales, not proof either facade satisfies the request. Freeze the actual approved opaque hero silhouette and recompute the minimum. Apply the user's >=2x requirement to map and practice walls and buildings; any lower cover exception needs explicit user/design approval and a clearly different identity. Compare side-by-side with the hero at 50/75/100% zoom; texture bounds alone do not prove apparent height.

Do not expand a collision rectangle to simulate tallness. Building data needs separate planar collision footprint, solid wall segments, door aperture/threshold, roof footprint, facade/roof height, depth-sort anchors, lighting occluders, LOS/projectile blockers and room ownership. Add deterministic room/portal IDs and versioned map/content identity. First implementation should be one continuous planar room with a real gap in the wall, one purposeful service, furniture blocking consistent with visible art, and an unambiguous exit. Decorative doors may remain only if visibly marked as nonenterable; no false doorway affordance. Multi-floor navigation, ladders, destructible buildings or seamless streamed indoor worlds are later scope, not accidental promises.

Before modifying the campus, choose and document whether physical projectiles and chemistry can cross an open doorway, whether closed doors block them, and what happens to active fields at thresholds. Preserve continuous-world authority where possible; if a room is a separate simulation space, room membership must enter snapshots, replay, spell ownership and loading admission. A door interaction cannot silently teleport into a different unsynchronized world. All peers must agree on transitions, door state and map identity. Include collision-at-jamb, diagonal entry, jump/wallrun, cast-through-door, simultaneous clients, reconnect indoors and loading cancellation.

Split information visibility from art occlusion. The local living viewpoint owner may remain readable through a front wall/roof using a silhouette or cutout. Friendly/hostile actors, effects, pickups and corpses are revealed only if gameplay visibility permits. Keep current owner-only body redraw; do not repaint the full scene through a transparent building. Cutaways should be limited to the obstructing foreground layer with hysteresis to prevent flicker, and should retain wall-foot contact/door boundaries. Spectating follows the current live observed owner, not the dead local body. Test tall wide walls, corners, stacked covers, air poses, camera movement,50/75/100% zoom, reduced motion, and enemies immediately behind the same obstacle. Current `los_cutaway` naming is not evidence of the requested new roof-removal behavior.

Godot's 2D lights and occluders can add depth, but light shadowing is not a replacement for collision or gameplay LOS. Use restrained baked/static light direction and contact shadows first; only a few high-value dynamic lights if measured budgets allow. Godot notes larger lights affect more pixels and cost more. [Godot 2D lights](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html)

**Loading, memory and lifetime.** Reuse the existing worker stream's immutable-byte snapshot, bounded pending ownership and main-thread publication rather than inventing generic asynchronous scene access. Godot warns that active scene access and shared mutable resources are not generally thread-safe; background loading can still block if retrieval is requested before readiness. [Thread-safe APIs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/thread_safe_apis.html), [background loading](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/io/background_loading.html). Preload only current/adjacent room and immediate actor actions, with a measured fallback/loading affordance. Never declare ready before collision, art, ownership and required textures all match the admitted map generation. Account for duplicate decoded buffers during verification. Test cancellation/reentry, travel pressure, invalid asset hash, shutdown during a worker task and memory returning to a bounded plateau over repeated room visits.

**Architecture and data ownership.** Reduce Bootstrap coupling in several small migrations after the slice contracts settle: (1) simulation/session coordinator; (2) presentation frame and scene composition; (3) room/terrain residency; (4) menu/focus/input coordinator; (5) profile/progression/release services. Prefer explicit small interfaces and typed values over a global event bus or generic framework. Keep gameplay calculations in simulation modules and keep presentation read-only. Do not move every dictionary into a new ECS merely to modernize the code. A change to floor height, door solidity, target eligibility or saved progression is an authority/schema change with independent review, protocol/content identity, migration and replay acceptance.

**Staging and stop conditions.**

1. Baseline and contracts: measure current ordinary/light/full packaged play, freeze hero scale, collision/visual height and visibility rules, declare minimum hardware and source/asset ownership. Exit when the top actual frame/memory constraints and the golden-slice acceptance matrix are agreed.
2. Golden slice: one complete hero, one projectile family, one explained AoE, one tall enterable building with one useful room, concise contextual UI. Compare offline rig-baked sprites versus 2D skeletal sprites with identical authority and the selected illustrated 2.5D target. Exit on human visual/feel acceptance plus budget, input, accessibility, save and two-client room proof. Reject attractive art that is too large to scale or hides gameplay.
3. Pipeline and architecture migration: complete the chosen import/animation/room contracts and small Bootstrap seams; keep the previous playable build. Expand three contrasting heroes and three spaces before thirty. Exit on coverage reports, stable lifetime/residency, no fallback core actions, exact gameplay migrations and reproducible build artifacts.
4. Content rollout and performance: scale the accepted asset patterns, simplify measured render/simulation costs, keep order/negative/tail checks, and compare whole player workloads. Do not compensate for missed frame targets by hiding paid effects, skipping timers or weakening authority.
5. Release qualification: focused tests and Full on exact final identity, package/archive/terrain, desktop lifecycle, earned save upgrade/recovery, actual controller/keyboard and two-machine sessions, frame/memory soak, clean-machine launch, native readability/accessibility review. Only then change the qualified pointer; preserve the prior playable fallback.

This is a complete direction with bounded gates, not an estimate that all stages fit in one pass. The biggest risk is simultaneous expansion of art volume, interiors and effects on a memory-constrained machine. The mitigation is a complete small slice with honest budgets, not another sequence of invisible micro-optimizations or a premature full-engine rewrite.
