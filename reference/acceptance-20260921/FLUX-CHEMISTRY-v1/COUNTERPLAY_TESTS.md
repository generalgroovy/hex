## 1. Harness and recording contract

Use the root-owned runnable checkout and test scheduler. Freeze source identity, working diff, actual ability definitions, chosen champion profiles and staged controls before starting. Execute baseline and each candidate separately; record exact commands, stable IDs, initial snapshot, fixed seed, expected predicate and observed result. A case passes only with recorded runtime evidence; leave unexecuted rows `NOT_RUN`, not “assumed pass.” [CONTRACT:L25-L41; AUDIT:L39-L41]

Use 120 Hz, 1000 world units per pixel, 1000 health units per HP and canonical axial centers `C(q,r)=(64000q+32000r,55425r)`. Hex vertices relative to center are `(0,-36950),(32000,-18475),(32000,18475),(0,36950),(-32000,18475),(-32000,-18475)`. Reuse the supplied exact selector/ledger, not a float-only surrogate. Negative coordinates and shared edges choose the same canonical owner as positive fixtures. [G:L7-L70]

End-to-end tests create each ingredient via reservation, payment and unique release source; never fabricate free spell branches. Unit geometry/height fixtures may isolate adapter inputs, but must be labelled **unit fixtures**, not evidence of a reachable player trajectory. Conservation tests need valid ledger partitions, including source IDs, flight/ground ownership and cumulative rounding; the missing local dependencies must be restored first. No test edits the real body sizes or gameplay stats to make results convenient. [W:L145-L188; W:L467-L511; W:L991-L1028]

For all ten pairs, exercise both input-element orderings, mirrored distinct-source inputs and mixed same-team/enemy-team ownership. Exercise every legal delivery path: ground deposit for all four families; continuous flight for Bolt/Heavy/Rapid combinations; Spray-versus-flight and same-phase Spray-versus-Spray. Do **not** invent a persistent Spray projectile or a Rapid trail just to fill a rectangular test matrix. Run strength combinations 1/3/5/15 with the authored family that actually provides each value; also test partial remainders. [P:L15-L73; P:L150-L157; C:L26-L134; W:L288-L365]

Record every case against baseline behavior first. Some baseline results are intentionally expected to expose a defect (e.g. D05, D16, D17). Preserve that evidence; only candidate-specific predicates change. Do not “fix” an expected result by changing the test to match the candidate.

## 3. Human drills — semantic actions, not guessed keybinds

Use the player's actual configured slots for the named family/element. Do not reintroduce old Q bindings or presume a particular uncompleted physical-attack migration. Each block below targets roughly 45–90 seconds of **active play**, not an estimated tool/runtime completion time. Repeat short blocks and swap roles until at least 12 counterbalanced opportunities per assessed condition are recorded. Leave outcome fields empty until played. Baseline and candidate footage must be clearly labelled.

For each opportunity record: source/experiment identity, reaction and family, paid Flux, size, floor/mode, setup, declared counter, command-eligibility tick, whether the counter succeeded, contact/damage/flow events and whether the player identified harmless expiry. A correct prediction is not automatically a successful escape; record both. [CONTRACT:L19-L27]

### H01 — Lay a lane; turn a lane (60–90 seconds)

**Setup:** Open lane with a marked destination and a side exit. Player A makes Flood with a deliberately aimed older source; then repeats using Vortex. Player B begins grounded outside. Swap who supplies the earlier ingredient; later add a wall-edge variation. No extra hazard for the first block.

**Action:** B says where movement will take them, crosses once grounded and once using a legal jump, then attempts the Vortex center path. A tries to use the same field to assist their own route. With E03, add opposing/parallel paid flows.

**Observe:** Can both players identify direction and applicability without color alone? Baseline Vortex center is **not** taught as safe; E01 must visibly expose actual excluded cells. Record intended route versus measured displacement, escape-command eligibility and wall pin duration. **Reject:** falsely safe center, unavoidable new wall pin, one body size requiring an unadvertised route, or loss of isolated-field movement under E03.

### H02 — Build cover that is worth using (60 seconds)

**Setup:** A paid Rampart across a firing line, with one deliberately open side route. Attacker uses an available Bolt, Heavy and Spray in separate repetitions; defender changes body size between blocks.

**Action:** Defender uses a verified wall action or side route. Attacker chooses “break,” “go around,” or “wait,” declares the choice, and tests one cover-destroying Spray pulse. Repeat near a protected spawn foot with escape space intentionally left open.

**Observe:** Shared cover health, face stability, break/expiry boundary, and whether the damaging shot is correctly stopped at the original surface. **Reject:** invisible live collider, same-pulse through-hit, trapped creation, or a “wall-jump surface” that cannot support the permitted movement. These are characterization failures, not reasons to increase health/area.

### H03 — Bait a burn; cross a pulse gap (45–60 seconds)

**Setup:** One Conflagration over a landing point; next two created one tick apart, then a bounded larger paid stack. Use baseline and E02 in separate labelled blocks.

**Action:** Defender calls “cross now,” “jump,” or “wait for decay,” then acts. Attacker tries to time a ground landing into a legitimate pulse, rather than repainting all space. Both must avoid their own active fire.

**Observe:** Actual pulse ticks, declared phase, successful crossings and harmful landings. E02 cannot be accepted just because aggregate damage fell: its global phase/first-pulse delay must be understandable. **Reject:** active cells read as harmless, no usable predicted crossing gap, or an attacker has no meaningful burn window despite correct setup.

### H04 — Mud is not Magma; Magma is not Firestorm (60–90 seconds)

**Setup:** Mark equal start/stop points. Present Mud, Magma and Firestorm separately with identical route intent; do not normalize their actual geometry by editing gameplay. Repeat a low- and high-strength Magma setup using the live paid costs.

**Action:** Defender first runs then brakes in Mud, next carries a slide through, then compares a ground/slide/jump/side-step across Magma and Firestorm. Say the predicted result before entry: “harder to stop,” “ground damage,” or “moving flame still reaches this jump.”

**Observe:** Mud start/stop response versus cruise speed, Magma damage while sliding, ordinary-speed penalty, and Firestorm airborne contact. E04 logs damage and utility per actual paid Flux and per pair. **Reject:** player is taught that sliding removes Magma damage or a low jump clears every flame; weak Magma becomes useless rather than a lower-commitment option.

### H05 — Leave, cool, heat; bait the screen (60–90 seconds)

**Setup:** First Steam alone, then a separate Mistcurrent firing lane. Use counterbalanced saved resets of valid paid state between blocks, not free matter injected into the active match. Show the observer actual exposure/intensity/capacity in the test overlay; hide it for the final recognition attempts.

**Action:** Defender crosses Steam briefly, stays longer once, then exits long enough to shed exposure. A new paid Water source cools it, then a new Fire source heats it in another reset; attempt a second same-channel modifier and one aimed at fading residue. In Mistcurrent, attacker deliberately spends real Fire to drain the finite screen and switches firing angle once it is spent.

**Observe:** Compare Steam pressure at equal exposure when evaluating cooling; otherwise exposure growth is a confound. In baseline, warn the tester that weak cooling can add scald—do not teach it as a safe counter. E05 must spend finite material without increased equal-E pressure or waste on decay. E06 must show lower-intensity **active** scald as dangerous, not expired. **Reject:** hidden added danger, free channel refresh, disappearing paid remainder, or an apparently full screen with no understandable spent-capacity state.

### H06 — Read the overlap without guessing from color (45–60 seconds)

**Setup:** Native scale, light then dark floor, normal then reduced effects. Present Fire+Steam, two Steam intensities, Conflagration+Steam, and Magma+Firestorm in short separate rounds with an actor, projectile and visible aim line. Add a Dustfront/Mistcurrent direction comparison.

**Action:** Defender identifies the exact affected hexes, whether the next threat is damage/steering/solid cover, a safe route, and when residue becomes harmless. Attacker calls which team may be hurt and which layer controls the next pressure winner.

**Observe:** Correct prediction versus actual source-attributed event; actor/projectile/aim readability; label individual remaining ambiguities. **Reject:** “all blue is safe,” “all friendly red is safe,” “every overlapping cell adds damage,” or Steam intensity borrowed from another source under E06. Do not fix failure by increasing opacity or particle count.

### H07 — Exit a contested spawn/station fairly (60–90 seconds)

**Setup:** Eight-player bounded lobby with all later-four content still enabled. Use an intentionally open spawn/station exit near a wall; pre-place finite first-four hazards through real paid casts. Repeat small/medium/large and attacker/defender roles; capture the protection1→0 boundary. Use actual station UI behavior.

**Action:** Defender selects and performs an available taught escape before protection ends. Attackers try two distinct paid setups: flow into a hazard and cover plus a timed ground pulse. No immediate free rearming or unlimited resources.

**Observe:** Protection, command eligibility, source payment, team damage, collider clearance and time pinned after a valid command. Distinguish a correct tactical catch from a repeated geometry/visibility lock that leaves no actionable response. **Reject:** hidden spawn geometry exclusion introduced only for the test, new displacement lock under E03, player-specific blocked escape, or a network lifecycle failure masked by ending the match early.
