# Reaction roles and delivery families
**Baseline: supplied source. Proposals are explicitly E01–E06; every experiment is NOT_RUN.**

## Reading the tables

One pixel is 1,000 world units; one HP is 1,000 health units; one second is 120 ticks. A ratio/intensity of 1,000 is 1.0. `I = min(1500, 100 × matched strength)`. Quantities are finite matter units, not HP or Flux. Geometry below selects **whole hexes** and is then stored as coverage; analytic radii do not clip later actor contact. References resolve through `evidence/SOURCE_REFERENCES.md`.

For all rows, setup means intersecting two **distinct, paid fundamental sources**. The same player may supply both, but reusing a branch of one source is not a new ingredient. Directional products use the older source record's aim, not automatically the second button press. Geometry/credit depends on stable source ordering as documented in the audit. ### Steam formula, without a fictitious minimum hit

For each actor, each tick: `E = min(240,E+1)` if any eligible active Steam was crossed; otherwise `E = max(0,E-2)`. For a product, take the maximum of `max(1,cell_quantity)` over its touched cells, not their sum. On `tick % 30 == 0`:

```
ramp   = 2000 + floor(6000 * E / 240)
normal = min(8000, floor(ramp * density / 400))
scald  = min(12000, floor(normal * intensity / 1000))
```

This competes with fundamental Fire in one strongest-pressure group. “2–12 HP per pulse” is not an accurate universal range: 2000 is the unscaled ramp origin, while cell density and intensity can make the resulting hit smaller. Leaving a capped exposure for 120 ticks (one second) clears it if no eligible Steam is crossed. Short crossings can legitimately miss a global pulse; a new Steam field does not reset personal exposure. [W:L861-L954]

## 4. Distinct choices to protect

**Flood versus Mistcurrent:** bidirectional-use route assistance with strength-scaled flow versus a long forward screen whose Fire interception stock can be baited out. **Dustfront versus Vortex:** lateral displacement across a lane versus ground rotation around a point, with E01's opening only after validation. **Mud versus Magma:** anticipating a stop/restart versus deciding whether to accept ground damage, jump, slide or detour. **Conflagration versus Firestorm:** stationary ground pulse timing versus a faster sweeping threat that reaches low airborne actors. **Steam versus all flame products:** manage accumulated exposure and paid thermal changes, not opacity or a generic damage carpet.

These distinctions are source-backed roles and test objectives, not a new suite of unpriced tertiary reactions. None requires a blanket increase in damage, radius, lifetime, caps or particle count.
