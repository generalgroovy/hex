# Mechanics audit and bounded optimization

Audited the current rules-6 resolver, spell catalogue, procedural generator, champion affinities, practice policy, planning previews and verification coverage. The audit started from source and prepared exchanges, rather than the previous release description. Historical rules engines and the map generator remain unchanged.

## Findings, decisions and acceptance

| Finding | Evidence and player consequence | Decision and acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certain allied movement conflicts were undisclosed | Two allies could select the same empty destination; `previewPlan` described both moves without warning, and both bounced. A stationary ally also blocks a chain of incoming allied moves. This wastes a turn on information already available to the player. | Add public-state advisory analysis in `lib/plan-insights.ts`. Tests prove duplicate and chained blocks, distinguish uncertain rival occupancy, and preserve valid allied swaps. Warnings remain advisory: players may deliberately accept a collision. |
| A planned cast could depend on a known failed allied landing | A spell may be valid from its intended destination but invalid from the retained origin after the allied block. | Report a certain fizzle for known allied blocks and a conditional fizzle for possible rival collisions. Use the same live `legalCasts` and `spellArea` functions as the resolver. Tests compare warnings against actual resolution. |
| Team-wide collateral needed more than direct-damage checks | Zero-damage Veil can Blind an ally; Vacuum can displace one. Warnings limited to damage miss those costs. | Analyze confirmed team spell footprints for damage, control and displacement. Use actual retained positions for known allied blocks, and describe exposure as conditional because rival movement remains hidden. Tests cover moving an ally clear and enemy-only Radiant Bolt safety. |
| Cauterize's cleanse exception omitted caster ownership | In a prepared exchange, friendly Clear Sight cleansed incoming Ember. Adding an enemy Cauterize at the same recipient incorrectly let Burn survive even though Cauterize only benefits friends. | Correct one predicate in the live resolver to require a friendly Cauterize. The regression also checks reversed order arrays, exchanged seats, strict restoration, friendly Cauterize's intended Burn cost and Wet extinguishing it. |
| Broad combat tests targeted historical engines | `tactics.test.ts` and `tactical-positions.test.ts` imported frozen v4; `v5-depth.test.ts` imported v5. The live suite strongly covered geometry but did not continuously enforce many inherited combat rules. | Add `v6-combat.test.ts`, preserving historical suites. It runs 26 live-engine cases: all 48 spell resolutions, all 15 reactions, aggregate mitigation, status timing, anti-lock protection, healing, theft, displacement, order independence, mirrored outcomes and simultaneous scoring. |

The Cauterize change is a correctness hotfix within current rules 6. Valid inputs, room protocol, save shape and generated maps do not change. Existing pending plans remain legal, but an unresolved exchange containing this specific enemy-Cauterize/cleanse combination will resolve with the corrected behavior after the server update. Frozen v3/v4/v5 engines preserve their prior behavior. A historical replay of that exact rules-6 edge case can therefore differ from the pre-hotfix result.

## Strategic questions retained for playtesting

The prior 120-game bot diagnostic equipped all 48 spells but never selected Backdraft, Tidal Surge, Deep Freeze, Raise Cairn, Veil or Reap. That policy had no dedicated barrier value and omitted conditional bonus damage from its spell score. This proved limited sparring coverage; it did not establish that those spells are weak against people. The subsequent interface cleanup adds existing-status bonus scoring for Backdraft, Deep Freeze and Reap, and equips the practice rival S. Wayne with Reap. Six behavioral tests cover the change; a six-match practice diagnostic selected Reap 16 times, all against already Withered targets. Barrier valuation and broader strategic search remain unimplemented. Avoid balance changes based only on those bots. Prepared human-facing challenges should compare successful use and counterplay before revising resource costs.

Eight groups of identical canonical affinity vectors contain 17 of the 30 identities. With equal health, Flux, movement and the same chosen loadout, champions in one group have identical combat behavior. This is a character differentiation question, rather than an engine bug. Adding passives would expand the rules and require separate balance evidence; this update preserves canonical affinities. Imported H.E.X. descriptions mention health, stamina or recovery distinctions that Confluence does not implement, so those descriptions should not be exposed as current mechanics.

Seed symmetry, reproducibility, bounded generation and immutable geography were sound in the reviewed implementation and existing tests. Geometric uniqueness is not a measure of strategic variety. The fixed number of wells and terrain cells may still produce similar strategic problems despite distinct layouts; observing human routes and contested objectives is needed to assess that.

## Verification and review

`node --experimental-strip-types --test tests/plan-insights.test.ts tests/v6-combat.test.ts` passed 33 tests. The experience and reliability agents independently reviewed the advisory helper; neither found a blocking issue. The reliability agent also reviewed the Cauterize ownership correction. The mechanics agent cross-reviewed the session recovery and exchange-feedback helpers, requesting that zero-point score events not produce the positive scoring sound.

The earlier 120-game and 48-game diagnostics remain evidence for their recorded source hashes. They are not fresh balance measurements of this hotfix. Automated checks establish specific correctness and recovery properties; they do not establish enjoyment, human balance or commercial potential.
